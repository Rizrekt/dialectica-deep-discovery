1. FORENSIC ACCOUNTING OF REAL CLAIMS (lead section). The verified corpus is entirely theoretical except PoW math. Present the claim process as an on-chain accounting exercise any verifier can replay from public chain data: (a) "free" is a claim about price, not about cost — the claimant pays the gas the claim transaction actually consumes at the gas price prevailing inside the FCFS window; the fee formula makes that cost scale one-for-one with the window's gas price, so the same claim shape is cheap in a quiet block and costs a large multiple of that when a contested window spikes the price, and the exact figure for any real window is decidable from its receipts (gas used × effective gas price, summed over successful claims); (b) reverted claims are not free either — failed attempts still burn the gas they consumed before the revert, so a botched or front-run claim wave imposes real cost on legitimate claimants that zero-price marketing never books, and that burn is visible in the same receipts; (c) a capped tranche offered first-come-first-served is consumed in a burst concentrated at the window open — claim transactions cluster in the first blocks of the window and the tranche's claimed counter moves in those same blocks; measure the depletion from the state changes across the window's blocks, not from the project's account of demand; (d) common-funder clustering: claimant wallets funded from one source are visible in the funding graph, which is where bot detection actually becomes possible — after the fact, forensically, not at the gate.
2. CLAIM-ACCOUNTING ILLUSIONS: WHAT BACKS THE CLAIM. Nobody in the verified set audits the asset side. A bundled reward is a promise whose backing sits somewhere, and whether a successful claim is worth anything is decidable from the distribution contract itself — never assert a specific contract's internals that you cannot point a verifier to; state the checks and what each outcome means: (a) where the reward is paid from a reserve held inside the mint contract, or otherwise under the issuer's control, enumerate the owner powers over that reserve — any withdrawal, rescue, or sweep path the owner retains over unclaimed rewards makes those entitlements revocable in substance: the gate can work perfectly while late claimants find the reward reserve drained. This is checkable by reading which functions can move the reserve and who may call them; (b) where the bundled asset is locked until a completion threshold (a sellout, a pool opening, a later unlock event) that may never occur, the claimant's balance reads full while realizable value is zero for exactly as long as the threshold stays unmet — the distribution succeeded as an accounting entry and failed as value transfer. The checkable discipline: before trusting any gated free mint, read the reserve's owner powers and the lock conditions, not just the gate.
3. SANCTIONED MULTI-IDENTITY (supporting section). The corpus frames multiple identities only as attack. The sharper point: issuance binds ACCOUNTS, not humans — a platform issues one Agent ID per account, and one operator legitimately running two issued identities (e.g. their own agent and a family member's agent they operate) is on-chain indistinguishable from a Sybil farm. "One per identity" therefore fails its distribution goal by design, with no rule broken and no attack to detect.
4. MEASURABLE SUCCESS CRITERION (close). Judge a gated mint not by claims blocked at the gate but by post-mint distribution: holder concentration after consolidation versus at mint, share of claims still holding at a later block. The gate's own logs cannot show this; only the transfer graph can.
