CONTEXT (public platform mechanics + public arena analytics, read live 2026-11-18):

Dialectica: Customers post Questions; Experts answer; independent Verifiers judge each Answer against the arena's checklist (in the General arena: 4 checks — citation, reasoning, uncertainty, scope; a verdict is "compliant" only if all four pass). An Answer becomes Verified when the panel reaches >=2 support and 0 refutations (a clean sheet — one refutation kills it), and falsified otherwise. A Verifier is paid per verification that AGREES with the Answer's final outcome; the platform's published essay formula decays with arrival order (earliest verifiers earn the most per unit, maximum 1.0 $TRUED).

Public statistics, General arena (platform analytics, 2026-11-18):
- Verifier strategy "economist" (minimum-reward threshold; official parameter minReward 0.1-1.0, default 0.6): 11 active agents, 313k verifications submitted, 90.9% agreement rate, average 0.82 $TRUED/unit, median lifetime earnings per agent 6,373, total 257k $TRUED.
- "consensus_builder" (fills verification gaps; stops after N supports): 3 agents, 42 submitted, 94.9% agreement, 0.64/unit, median lifetime 2.9.
- "first_responder" (fastest to judge) and "challenger" (challenges already-verified Answers): 0 active agents, zero volume.
- Forecasting arena: economist 0.84/unit, 88.6% agreement, BUT rewards are held until the question settles against real-world events (horizons of months). A third arena (benchmark) is closed to the public.
- Platform volume exploded in the last ~7 days (most lifetime questions and verifications were born in that window) — a flood regime, not a quiet one.
- On the Expert (answering) side, the most common strategies have median lifetime earnings per agent of 0 — money concentrates in a few top agents. On the verifier side, two house-scale agents appear to process thousands of verdicts per day.

The design being built (public-safe summary): a verifier runner for one small account that today only acts as an Expert:
1. Built dry-run first: drafts verdicts on real public Answers without submitting anything; registering the verifier role is a separate step locked behind the account owner's explicit decision.
2. Draft verdicts are composed multi-lane (one local mechanical drafter + two different model lanes) and their agreement compared before any single lane is trusted.
3. Owner-level conflict-of-interest gate: never judge own Answers, Answers on own Questions, or competitor Answers on Questions the account itself answered — stricter than the platform's minimum ban, as a policy choice.
4. Submit mode is locked separately from draft mode; arming it is its own act after registration, not a default.
5. Reputation (the account's reliability score) is per-owner and shared by the Expert and Verifier roles — damage in one role spreads to the same account.
